    Method

    The model in three equations. Everything on this page is computed from them.

    Orders arrive less often far from the mid

    λ(δ) = A e−kδ

    Market orders that would hit a quote at distance δ arrive as a Poisson process. In one step of length dt the quote fills with probability 1 − e−λ(δ)dt, one unit at a time.

    Each fill earns the distance from the mid

    E[spread income] = 2 N P(fill) δ

    Income per side, δ λ(δ), is largest at δ = 1/k = 0.50. The closed-form value is checked against every run above.

    Inventory adds risk, not return

    Var(∫ q dS) = σ² ∫ E[qt²] dt

    With no drift and no adverse selection, holding inventory has zero expected value. It only adds variance, which grows with σ² and with how far inventory wanders.

    What the model leaves out

    • Adverse selection. Orders here never anticipate price moves. Real ones often do, which makes inventory costlier and flatters the fixed spread.
    • Closing costs. Leftover inventory is valued at the mid with no cost to unwind it.
    • Market frictions. No fees, rebates, latency or queue position; fills happen exactly at the quote.
    • Fat tails. Prices follow a driftless random walk with constant volatility.

    Everything on this page is simulated. Prices and orders are generated by the model, so these numbers describe the model, not any real market.
